# Meta Ads Creative Strategy — AI WhatsApp Ordering Agent for Restaurants & Cafés

*Prepared for: B2B lead generation campaign targeting restaurant/café owners and decision-makers*
*Product reference: "Tandoori Agent" demo build — to be positioned as a customizable, restaurant-agnostic AI solution*

---

## 1. Executive Recommendation

Sell **staff hours saved and orders captured**, not "AI." Restaurant owners buy this product because it answers WhatsApp instantly, never misses an order during rush, and works while they sleep — not because it's "AI-powered." The word "AI" should appear as a *credibility signal*, never as the headline benefit.

Lead with the single most visceral, universal pain point every restaurant/café owner has personally lived: **the phone/WhatsApp is ringing during rush hour and nobody can answer it — so the order goes to a competitor.** Every other benefit (upselling, multi-branch, menu photo upload, 24/7 availability) is secondary and should be sequenced in based on awareness stage.

Core recommendation: run a **problem-first, demo-second** creative system. Open every ad on a recognizable operational pain (missed WhatsApp orders, overwhelmed staff, closed-hours inquiries), resolve it visually by showing the actual WhatsApp conversation flow (this product's single most persuasive proof asset), and close on a low-friction offer — a free custom demo built on *their own menu*, not a generic trial.

The Tandoori-branded demo should be shown transparently, but always framed as one live example among many possible builds — "this happens to be a karahi restaurant — yours could be a café, bakery, or burger shop, same engine underneath."

## 2. Target-Customer Insights

### Biggest operational and sales problems
- **Missed orders during peak hours.** One or two staff can't handle phone + WhatsApp + in-store customers simultaneously — inquiries get left on read or never answered.
- **Repetitive, low-value staff work.** Staff spend hours a day retyping the same menu answers, repeating delivery addresses, and manually confirming order status.
- **Order errors from miscommunication.** Handwritten or verbally-taken WhatsApp orders lead to wrong items, wrong addresses, and disputes.
- **No coverage after hours.** Customers messaging late at night or during off-hours get no response and simply order from a competitor who does reply.
- **Inconsistent customer experience.** Response quality and speed depends entirely on which staff member happens to be free.
- **No visibility into what's actually happening.** Owners often don't know their peak hours, best-selling items, or repeat-customer rate — they're running blind.
- **Multi-branch chaos.** Owners with more than one location struggle to standardize menus, hours, and order handling across branches.

### Most emotionally compelling pain points
1. **Lost revenue they can *see* happening** — "I know we're losing orders every night after 11pm, I just can't watch WhatsApp that long."
2. **The stress of rush hour** — phones buzzing, WhatsApp piling up, a line at the counter, and no way to be in three places at once.
3. **Feeling behind competitors** who already reply instantly or take orders more smoothly.
4. **Guilt/frustration over hiring** — needing to hire and train more staff just to keep up with messages, when margins are already thin.

### Strongest buying motivations
- Capturing orders they are currently losing (direct revenue).
- Reducing headcount/labor cost pressure without reducing service quality.
- Looking modern and professional compared to competitors still doing manual WhatsApp order-taking.
- Freeing existing staff to focus on food quality and in-store service instead of typing out orders.
- Peace of mind — "it just works, even when I'm not watching."

### Common objections
- *"My customers won't want to talk to a bot."* → Counter with: natural language understanding (English, Roman Urdu, Urdu script, voice notes), human handoff whenever the AI can't help or the customer asks for one.
- *"It's too complicated / too much tech for me to manage."* → Counter with: menu setup via a single photo, live dashboard, less-than-a-day setup.
- *"It won't understand my menu / my customers' way of talking."* → Counter with: Roman Urdu + Urdu script + voice note support, fuzzy/smart search, semantic search understands "meetha," "spicy," "cold drink," etc.
- *"What if it gets an order wrong?"* → Counter with: price verification against live menu, delivery zone/minimum validation, duplicate protection, never guesses unclear prices.
- *"I don't want to lose the personal touch."* → Counter with: AI personality settings (e.g., "Warm & Professional"), seamless human handoff on request or frustration detection.
- *"Is this only for [specific cuisine]?"* → Counter directly: the product adapts to any menu, 10 items or 200, desi food, fast food, café, multi-cuisine.
- *"Sounds expensive / not worth it for a small place."* → Address with a low-friction offer (free demo on their own menu) rather than a hard price claim, since no pricing data was supplied.

### Audience awareness level
Mostly **problem-aware, not solution-aware.** Most restaurant/café owners know they're losing orders and struggling with WhatsApp volume, but have not necessarily connected that problem to "an AI ordering assistant" as the category of fix. A smaller segment (used to seeing chatbots in other industries) is **solution-aware** but skeptical of quality/fit. Very few are **product-aware** of this specific offering. This means top-of-funnel creative must do problem-agitation before introducing the mechanism, while remarketing creative can go straight to product demonstration.

### Benefits to emphasize first (in order)
1. Never miss a WhatsApp order — even during rush hour or after closing.
2. Customers order in their own words, in their own language — no app download.
3. Orders arrive accurate, organized, and ready for the kitchen — no manual retyping.
4. Automatic upsell prompts increase order value.
5. One live dashboard to manage everything — orders, chats, menu, staff.
6. Setup takes less than a day; menu goes live from a single photo.

## 3. Core Positioning and Offer

**Positioning statement (one sentence):**
"[Product] is a WhatsApp ordering assistant that answers every customer instantly, takes accurate orders around the clock, and hands off to your staff only when a human touch is needed — built around your menu, for any restaurant or café."

**Strongest overall value proposition:**
Restaurants stop losing orders to unanswered WhatsApp messages and overworked staff, without hiring anyone new — because the assistant handles ordering conversations 24/7, in the customer's own language, and only escalates to a human when it genuinely needs to.

### Messaging angles (ranked)

1. **"Never miss another WhatsApp order"** (loss-aversion / missed-revenue angle)
   Hook: *"How many WhatsApp messages did your restaurant not answer last night?"*

2. **"Your busiest hour, handled"** (rush-hour relief angle)
   Hook: *"7pm. Kitchen's slammed. WhatsApp's exploding. Who's answering?"*

3. **"It talks the way your customers actually talk"** (trust/fit angle — English, Roman Urdu, Urdu script, voice notes)
   Hook: *"Mujhe 2 chicken karahi chahiye" — and it understood perfectly.*

4. **"From printed menu to live ordering in one photo"** (ease-of-adoption angle)
   Hook: *"Take one photo of your menu. That's the whole setup."*

5. **"One system for every branch"** (growth/multi-location angle, for larger operators)
   Hook: *"3 branches. 1 dashboard. Zero WhatsApp chaos."*

### Feature sequencing
**In the ad (first 15–30 seconds):** instant WhatsApp response, natural-language ordering, order accuracy, 24/7 availability, upsell prompts, human handoff.
**On landing page / in demo call:** multi-branch management, analytics dashboard, day/time-based menu scheduling, staff roles & permissions, native WhatsApp Flows, delivery zone/minimum logic, security/duplicate protection, order status notifications.

### Calls to action
- "Get a free demo built on your own menu"
- "See it work on WhatsApp — no obligation"
- "Book a 15-minute walkthrough"
- "Message us to try it as a customer would"

### Recommended low-friction offer
**Free custom demo, built on the advertiser's own actual menu (uploaded as a photo), delivered as a live WhatsApp conversation they can test themselves before ever speaking to a salesperson.** This mirrors the product's own "menu photo → live in a day" mechanic, making the offer itself a proof point, and it's fully supported by the feature list (menu photo upload, fast setup).

## 4. Recommended Creative Formats, Ranked by Priority

### 1. Screen-recording / WhatsApp conversation demo ads — **highest priority**
- **Why it works:** This product's single strongest proof asset is the actual WhatsApp conversation. Nothing sells "it understands natural language and handles orders smoothly" like watching it happen in the real interface people already trust.
- **Awareness stage:** Problem-aware → solution-aware (mid-funnel), also works cold with a strong pain-point hook first.
- **Ideal duration/size:** 15–30 sec, 9:16 for Reels/Stories, 1:1 as backup for feed.
- **Opening hook:** A messy/missed-message scenario, then cut to the smooth AI conversation.
- **Visual structure:** Phone-screen recording of a real (or realistic) WhatsApp chat — greeting → menu browse → order → upsell prompt → confirmation — sped up with captions calling out each capability.
- **Main message:** "This is what your customers experience — instantly, every time."
- **CTA:** "Try it yourself" / "Get a free demo on your menu."
- **Show on screen:** Real interactive buttons, order summary, upsell prompt, delivery confirmation.
- **Avoid:** Overlaying too much text on top of the chat; let the conversation breathe.

### 2. Problem-solution short-form video ads — **high priority**
- **Why it works:** Meets cold, problem-aware audiences where they are; agitates a felt pain before introducing the fix.
- **Awareness stage:** Unaware/problem-aware (top of funnel, cold audiences).
- **Ideal duration/size:** 15–20 sec, 9:16 vertical.
- **Opening hook:** Visual of a phone buzzing non-stop / a staff member overwhelmed at the counter with WhatsApp notifications piling up.
- **Visual structure:** Chaos (3–5 sec) → calm resolution as the AI handles it (10 sec) → simple CTA card (3 sec).
- **Main message:** "You're losing orders you never even see. Here's what stops that."
- **CTA:** "See how it works — free demo."
- **Show on screen:** Notification pile-up, then a single clean WhatsApp exchange.
- **Avoid:** Overacted "stressed owner" stock footage that feels staged; keep it grounded and specific.

### 3. Restaurant-owner testimonial-style videos — **high priority (once available)**
- **Why it works:** Peer credibility is extremely persuasive to small-business owners who trust other operators over marketing claims.
- **Awareness stage:** Solution-aware, comparison-shopping.
- **Ideal duration/size:** 20–30 sec, 9:16.
- **Opening hook:** Owner speaking directly to camera about the specific problem they had.
- **Visual structure:** Talking-head problem statement → cut to their WhatsApp/dashboard in use → closing endorsement line.
- **Main message:** Real operator, real relief from a specific pain (never invent quotes — use only real client statements once available).
- **CTA:** "Talk to us about your restaurant."
- **Show on screen:** Real face, real dashboard/chat, restaurant setting.
- **Avoid:** Scripted-sounding lines, unverifiable performance claims, exaggerated results.
*(Note: use only once real testimonials exist — do not fabricate.)*

### 4. Carousel ads (feature walkthrough) — **medium priority**
- **Why it works:** Good for retargeting warm audiences who need more detail before booking a call; lets you sequence multiple proof points without overloading a single frame.
- **Awareness stage:** Mid-funnel remarketing.
- **Ideal size:** 1:1 or 4:5, 4–6 cards.
- **Opening hook (card 1):** "One system. Every part of your ordering, handled."
- **Visual structure:** Each card = one feature screenshot (chat, dashboard, menu upload, order status) with a one-line caption.
- **Main message:** Breadth of capability without a cluttered single image.
- **CTA:** Final card — "Book your free demo."
- **Avoid:** Cramming feature lists into card copy; one idea per card.

### 5. Static image ads (dashboard/chat mockups) — **medium priority**
- **Why it works:** Cheap to produce and test at volume, strong for retargeting and for simple, single-message hooks.
- **Awareness stage:** All stages, best for retargeting and quick A/B testing of hooks.
- **Ideal size:** 1:1 and 4:5.
- **Opening hook:** Bold headline over a phone mockup showing a WhatsApp order in progress.
- **Visual structure:** Phone mockup center-frame, headline above, CTA button below.
- **Main message:** One clear benefit statement.
- **CTA:** "Get a free demo."
- **Avoid:** Busy backgrounds, more than one message per image.

### 6. Product-demonstration videos (dashboard walkthrough) — **medium priority**
- **Why it works:** Builds trust for owners who are more operationally minded and want to see the "backend," not just the customer-facing chat.
- **Awareness stage:** Solution-aware, later funnel.
- **Ideal duration:** 30–45 sec, 1:1 or 16:9 for feed.
- **Opening hook:** "Here's what you see when an order comes in."
- **Visual structure:** Screen recording of dashboard — live order feed → status update → customer notification.
- **Main message:** Control and visibility, not just automation.
- **CTA:** "See your dashboard — book a walkthrough."
- **Avoid:** Narrating every single setting; focus on 2–3 dashboard moments.

### 7. Founder/expert-style ads — **medium priority**
- **Why it works:** Builds trust through direct, credible explanation; performs well for B2B software buyers who want to understand *why*, not just *what*.
- **Awareness stage:** Unaware to solution-aware.
- **Ideal duration:** 30–45 sec, 9:16 or 1:1.
- **Opening hook:** Founder speaking directly to camera: "Every restaurant owner I talk to has the same problem…"
- **Visual structure:** Talking head intercut with product screen recordings.
- **Main message:** Direct, credible explanation of the problem and the fix.
- **CTA:** "Message us to see it live."
- **Avoid:** Overly polished corporate tone; keep it conversational.

### 8. UGC-style ads — **lower-medium priority**
- **Why it works:** Feels native to the feed, lower guard from viewers, good for cold prospecting once scripted well.
- **Awareness stage:** Cold, top-of-funnel.
- **Ideal duration:** 15–20 sec, 9:16.
- **Opening hook:** Casual, handheld-style delivery: "If you run a restaurant and you're still manually replying to WhatsApp orders…"
- **Visual structure:** Direct-to-camera talking point → quick cut to chat demo.
- **Main message:** Relatable, informal framing of the core pain and fix.
- **CTA:** "Check it out — link below."
- **Avoid:** Making it feel like a paid actor read a script; keep language natural.

### 9. Before-and-after workflow ads — **lower priority**
- **Why it works:** Simple visual contrast is easy to grasp instantly, good scroll-stopper.
- **Awareness stage:** Cold.
- **Ideal size:** Split-screen static or short video, 1:1.
- **Opening hook:** Split screen — "Before" (missed messages, messy notes) vs "After" (clean AI chat).
- **Visual structure:** Literal before/after split.
- **Main message:** Instant visual contrast of chaos vs. order.
- **CTA:** "See the after for your restaurant."
- **Avoid:** Making the "before" side look like a caricature; keep it realistic.

### 10. Motion-graphics ads — **lowest priority**
- **Why it works:** Useful for explaining the *system* (multi-branch, dashboard architecture) but less persuasive than real product footage for this audience.
- **Awareness stage:** Mid-funnel, more analytical/operator-minded segment (multi-branch owners).
- **Ideal duration:** 20–30 sec, 1:1.
- **Opening hook:** Animated diagram: "One WhatsApp number. Every branch."
- **Visual structure:** Simple animated flow diagram.
- **Main message:** System clarity for operationally-minded, multi-branch buyers.
- **CTA:** "Talk to us about multi-branch setup."
- **Avoid:** Overly abstract animation with no real product visuals — this audience trusts what looks real over what looks slick.

## 5. Six Detailed Creative Concepts

### Concept 1 — "The Messages You Never Answered" (Pain-point-focused)
- **Target segment:** Single-location restaurant/café owners, high WhatsApp order volume.
- **Pain point:** Missed orders during rush hour and after hours.
- **Angle:** Loss aversion — revenue lost, not gained.
- **First 3-sec hook:** Phone screen showing 14 unread WhatsApp messages, notification sound.
- **Headline:** "How many of these did you actually answer?"
- **Supporting copy:** "Every unread WhatsApp message is a customer who might have ordered from your competitor instead. An AI ordering assistant answers instantly — day or night — so you stop losing orders you never even see."
- **Visual concept:** Real-feeling phone screen recording of a notification pile-up, then a calm reveal of the AI replying instantly.
- **Scene-by-scene:**
  1. (0–3s) Phone buzzing repeatedly, unread badges stacking up.
  2. (3–8s) Cut to same phone number, but now every message has an instant reply — order details flowing smoothly.
  3. (8–15s) Order summary appears, confirmation sent, kitchen notified.
  4. (15–20s) Text card: "Never miss another order."
- **Voiceover/on-screen text:** "This many missed messages. This many missed orders." → "Now imagine every single one, answered instantly."
- **CTA:** "Get a free demo on your own menu."
- **Placement/aspect ratio:** Reels/Stories, 9:16.
- **Why it may perform well:** Universally recognizable pain, strong pattern interrupt with the notification stack visual.
- **Risks:** Needs a believable, non-staged-looking phone mockup or it can feel gimmicky.

---

### Concept 2 — "One Photo. Live in a Day." (Product-demonstration)
- **Target segment:** Owners hesitant about tech complexity — smaller independent restaurants/cafés.
- **Pain point:** Fear that setup is complicated or time-consuming.
- **Angle:** Ease of adoption.
- **First 3-sec hook:** A hand photographing a physical printed menu with a phone.
- **Headline:** "This is the whole setup."
- **Supporting copy:** "No manual data entry. No complicated software. Just take a photo of your existing menu, and your AI ordering assistant is ready to take orders on WhatsApp — often within a day."
- **Visual concept:** Screen-recording sequence: photo taken → uploaded to dashboard → menu appears digitized → toggled live.
- **Scene-by-scene:**
  1. (0–3s) Hand snaps photo of a real printed/handwritten menu.
  2. (3–10s) Dashboard screen recording: photo uploads, items auto-populate with prices and categories.
  3. (10–18s) Toggle switch flips to "Accepting Orders" — cut to a customer immediately ordering on WhatsApp.
  4. (18–25s) Text card: "From menu photo to live orders — that fast."
- **Voiceover/on-screen text:** "Take a photo." → "Menu goes live." → "Customers start ordering."
- **CTA:** "See it built on your menu — free."
- **Placement/aspect ratio:** Feed + Reels, 1:1 and 9:16 versions.
- **Why it may perform well:** Directly defuses the #1 objection (complexity) with visual proof, not just a claim.
- **Risks:** Needs a real or highly realistic menu-photo-to-digital-menu demo to be credible — a fake-looking mockup will undercut the message.

---

### Concept 3 — "It Understood Exactly What I Meant" (Outcome-focused / trust)
- **Target segment:** Owners serving a Roman Urdu / Urdu-speaking or mixed-language customer base.
- **Pain point:** Fear that automated systems won't understand how customers actually talk.
- **Angle:** Natural-language fit and trust.
- **First 3-sec hook:** Text bubble appears: "Mujhe 2 chicken karahi aur 4 naan chahiye."
- **Headline:** "It speaks the way your customers actually type."
- **Supporting copy:** "English, Roman Urdu, Urdu script, even voice notes — the AI understands natural, everyday language, not just exact commands. So every customer gets a smooth ordering experience, in their own words."
- **Visual concept:** Split-screen or sequential chat bubbles showing the same order placed in English, then Roman Urdu, then Urdu script, then as a voice note — each getting a correct, smooth response.
- **Scene-by-scene:**
  1. (0–4s) English order typed, AI responds correctly.
  2. (4–9s) Roman Urdu order typed, AI responds correctly.
  3. (9–14s) Voice note sent, waveform animates, AI transcribes and responds.
  4. (14–20s) Text card: "However your customers talk, it understands."
- **Voiceover/on-screen text:** Captions of each message type as it's shown.
- **CTA:** "Try it in your own words — free demo."
- **Placement/aspect ratio:** Reels/Stories, 9:16.
- **Why it may perform well:** Directly addresses a strong, specific local objection with concrete visual proof — differentiates from generic Western chatbot ads.
- **Risks:** Must feel authentic — real phrasing, not stiff or translated-sounding text.

---

### Concept 4 — "Ask the Person Who Built It" (Founder/expert-style)
- **Target segment:** Broad — owners who want to understand the "why" before trusting the "what."
- **Pain point:** Skepticism about AI / distrust of unfamiliar tech vendors.
- **Angle:** Direct, credible explanation from the founder.
- **First 3-sec hook:** Founder, direct to camera: "Every restaurant owner I talk to loses orders on WhatsApp. Here's why."
- **Headline:** "We built this because restaurants kept losing orders on WhatsApp."
- **Supporting copy:** "This isn't a generic chatbot. It's built specifically to take orders, answer menu questions, and hand off to your staff when it should — for any restaurant or café, not just one type of food."
- **Visual concept:** Talking-head founder segments intercut with quick product screen recordings illustrating each point made.
- **Scene-by-scene:**
  1. (0–5s) Founder states the core problem directly.
  2. (5–20s) Cut to chat/dashboard clips illustrating each capability mentioned (instant reply, human handoff, order accuracy).
  3. (20–30s) Founder: "It's built for any menu — desi food, fast food, café, anything."
  4. (30–35s) Text card + CTA.
- **Voiceover/on-screen text:** Founder's own dialogue, minimal captions reinforcing key phrases.
- **CTA:** "Message us — let's build yours."
- **Placement/aspect ratio:** Feed, 1:1 or 4:5; 9:16 cutdown for Reels.
- **Why it may perform well:** Founder-led ads build outsized trust for B2B SaaS-style purchases from small business owners who are wary of faceless software vendors.
- **Risks:** Delivery must feel authentic and unscripted, not corporate — a stiff read will undercut the trust it's meant to build.

### Concept 5 — "Built for a Karahi Place. Works for Any Kitchen." (Curiosity-based / broad positioning)
- **Target segment:** Broad prospecting — all restaurant/café types, explicitly countering the "this is just for desi food" assumption.
- **Pain point:** Uncertainty about whether the product fits their specific type of food business.
- **Angle:** Curiosity + category-agnostic positioning.
- **First 3-sec hook:** Fast cuts of different food types — karahi, burger, coffee, biryani, pizza — each with the same WhatsApp chat overlay.
- **Headline:** "One AI ordering assistant. Every kind of kitchen."
- **Supporting copy:** "We built this live for a desi restaurant — but the engine underneath works for cafés, fast food, bakeries, and cloud kitchens too. Same instant WhatsApp ordering, built around your menu."
- **Visual concept:** Quick-cut montage of different cuisines/food business types, each shown with the identical chat interface adapting to a different menu.
- **Scene-by-scene:**
  1. (0–3s) Karahi dish + chat bubble "2 chicken karahi please."
  2. (3–6s) Burger + chat bubble "1 cheeseburger, no onions."
  3. (6–9s) Coffee + chat bubble "1 large latte, oat milk."
  4. (9–14s) Text overlay: "Different menus. Same smart ordering system."
  5. (14–18s) CTA card.
- **Voiceover/on-screen text:** "Karahi. Burgers. Coffee. Same system, your menu."
- **CTA:** "See what it looks like for your business."
- **Placement/aspect ratio:** Reels/Stories, 9:16.
- **Why it may perform well:** Directly solves the brief's core positioning risk (avoiding "Tandoori-only" perception) while using pattern-interrupt variety to hold attention.
- **Risks:** Needs genuinely distinct-looking food/menu visuals for each category or it can feel repetitive rather than varied.

### Concept 6 — "Try It Yourself, Free" (Offer-focused)
- **Target segment:** Owners who are problem-aware and comparison-shopping but haven't converted yet (strong for retargeting).
- **Pain point:** Uncertainty about whether it'll actually work for *their* specific menu and customers.
- **Angle:** Risk-reversal via a free, personalized demo.
- **First 3-sec hook:** Bold on-screen text: "We'll build this for your menu. Free."
- **Headline:** "See it working on your own menu — before you decide anything."
- **Supporting copy:** "Send us a photo of your menu. We'll set up a live WhatsApp demo you can test yourself — no cost, no obligation. If it doesn't feel right for your restaurant, no hard feelings."
- **Visual concept:** Simple, clean static or short motion graphic: menu photo → phone mockup with chat → "Your demo, ready" card.
- **Scene-by-scene:**
  1. (0–3s) Hand holding a menu, text: "Send us a photo."
  2. (3–8s) Phone mockup shows a demo chat forming with items from that exact menu.
  3. (8–13s) Text card: "Free. No obligation. Yours to test."
  4. (13–16s) CTA card.
- **Voiceover/on-screen text:** "Your menu. Your demo. Free to try."
- **CTA:** "Send your menu — get your free demo."
- **Placement/aspect ratio:** Feed + Stories, 1:1 and 9:16.
- **Why it may perform well:** Removes the biggest purchase friction (uncertainty about fit) with a concrete, personalized, zero-risk next step.
- **Risks:** Requires operational capacity to actually deliver personalized demos quickly, or it can create a poor first impression if response is slow.

## 6. AI Production Workflow

| Stage | Recommended approach | Tool suggestions | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scriptwriting | AI-assisted drafts, human edited for tone/local phrasing | Claude / ChatGPT for first drafts | Always have a human pass for natural Pakistani-market phrasing and accuracy against the real feature list. |
| Storyboarding | Simple shot-list docs, not elaborate boards | Google Slides / Figma / Canva | Speed over polish — these are short-form ads. |
| AI voiceover | Use for VO-only demo/explainer ads | ElevenLabs | Choose a natural, warm, non-robotic voice; avoid generic "AI narrator" tone. |
| AI avatars/presenters | Use sparingly — only for lower-priority explainer or motion-graphics style ads | HeyGen / Synthesia | **Prefer a real founder or real staff member on camera** for founder-style and testimonial-style ads — this audience responds far better to real people than AI avatars, and an avatar undercuts trust for a *trust-sensitive* purchase. |
| Motion graphics | AI-assisted asset generation, human-assembled | Canva, After Effects (with AI plugins), Figma | Keep animation simple and clean — avoid generic template looks. |
| Interface mockups | **Use real screen recordings of the actual product wherever possible** | Native screen recording (phone + dashboard) | This is the single highest-leverage asset category — real product footage will always out-convert a fabricated mockup for a "does this actually work" audience. |
| WhatsApp conversation simulations | Real screen recordings preferred; AI-generated only as backup for concepts not yet built | Screen recording tools, or if simulated: Figma WhatsApp UI kits | If simulated, must be pixel-accurate to real WhatsApp UI or it reads as fake and damages trust. |
| Restaurant footage | Real footage strongly preferred over AI-generated | Phone footage from actual/partner restaurants, or licensed stock as fallback | Real kitchen/counter footage adds authenticity that stock or AI video cannot match for this audience. |
| Image generation | Fine for supporting graphics (icons, backgrounds, text cards) | Midjourney / DALL·E / Canva AI | Do not use AI-generated images to fake "customer" or "restaurant owner" photos — feels inauthentic and risks misrepresentation. |
| Video generation | Use only for abstract/motion-graphics segments, not for people or product footage | Runway / Pika | Avoid using generative video for anything meant to look like a real customer, real restaurant, or real product screen. |
| Editing | Standard short-form editing, fast cuts, captions burned in | CapCut / Premiere Pro | Optimize first 3 seconds obsessively — that's where retention is won or lost. |
| Captions | Always on, burned-in, high contrast | CapCut auto-caption + manual cleanup | Most viewers watch with sound off — captions are not optional. |
| Background music | Light, modern, non-distracting | Epidemic Sound / Artlist | Keep music under dialogue/VO; avoid generic "corporate tech" music beds that feel templated. |
| Resizing for placements | Always produce 9:16, 4:5, and 1:1 versions | Canva / CapCut auto-resize, manual crop check | Manually check that key text/UI isn't cropped out in each ratio — don't rely purely on auto-resize. |

**Overall principle:** Use AI tools to speed up production (scripts, voiceover, editing, graphics), but keep the *proof* elements — the actual WhatsApp chat, the actual dashboard, the actual founder or staff — as real, unfabricated footage. For a product whose core sales pitch is "trustworthy and it actually works," AI-generated avatars or fabricated product footage would undermine the exact credibility the campaign is trying to build.

## 8. A/B Testing Matrix

### Testing priority order (test in this sequence)
1. **Hooks** (first 3 seconds) — highest leverage on thumb-stop rate.
2. **Pain point / angle** — determines who stops and who scrolls.
3. **Offer framing** — free demo vs. free consultation vs. "test it yourself" language.
4. **Static vs. video** — format-level spend allocation.
5. **Product demo vs. emotional problem-solution creative** — messaging style.
6. **Real founder/staff vs. AI avatar or motion graphics** — trust/production style.
7. **Short vs. detailed copy** — primary text length.
8. **Broad restaurant messaging vs. segment-specific messaging** (e.g., café-specific vs. desi-restaurant-specific vs. generic).

### Testing matrix (minimum viable structure)

| Angle | Hook A | Hook B | Visual Execution 1 | Visual Execution 2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Missed Orders (pain)** | "How many WhatsApp messages did your restaurant not answer last night?" | "7pm. Kitchen's slammed. Who's answering WhatsApp?" | Screen-recording notification pile-up | Problem-solution short video |
| **Ease of Setup (adoption)** | "This is the whole setup — one photo of your menu." | "Still typing your menu into a system by hand?" | Screen-recording menu upload demo | Static image: menu photo → phone mockup |
| **Trust/Language Fit** | "Mujhe 2 chicken karahi chahiye — and it understood perfectly." | "It talks the way your customers actually type." | Multi-language chat demo video | Carousel: EN / Roman Urdu / Urdu script / voice note cards |

*(Expand this same structure to the remaining two angles — "Broad Fit / Any Restaurant" and "Free Demo Offer" — once initial data comes in, to reach 5 angles × 2 hooks × 2 executions = 20 initial ad variants.)*

### Method for identifying winning concepts
- Run all variants at equal minimum spend for 3–5 days before judging (avoid early-stopping bias).
- **Primary funnel metric to optimize toward:** cost per qualified lead (demo request / WhatsApp conversation / booked call) — not just cost per click or CTR.
- **Secondary diagnostic metrics:**
  - Thumb-stop rate / 3-second video views → diagnoses hook strength.
  - Hold rate to 50%/75% → diagnoses whether the middle of the ad is losing people.
  - CTR → diagnoses whether the offer/CTA is compelling once attention is earned.
  - Cost per lead + lead quality (from actual sales conversations) → the real success metric.
- Declare a winner only once a variant shows a statistically meaningful gap in cost-per-qualified-lead, not just cheaper cost-per-click.

### What to improve when an ad gets attention but no leads
- **High hook/hold rate, low CTR:** the offer or CTA isn't compelling enough, or the mid-ad message doesn't connect the pain to the fix clearly — tighten the middle section and clarify the CTA.
- **High CTR, low lead conversion (on landing page/WhatsApp/form):** the destination experience is the problem, not the ad — check landing page clarity, form friction, or WhatsApp opening message quality.
- **Good conversion, poor lead quality (unqualified leads):** the messaging is too broad or the offer is too generic — tighten targeting language (e.g., explicitly mention "restaurant/café owners") and consider adding a light qualifying question in the instant form or WhatsApp opener.
- **Low hook/hold rate overall:** the visual isn't earning attention in the first 3 seconds — test a more specific, concrete pain visual (real notification pile-up, real chat) over abstract or generic footage.

## 9. Campaign and Funnel Recommendations

**Meta campaign objective:** Leads objective (using Meta's lead-gen tools) or Sales/Conversions objective if driving to a landing page with tracked demo-request conversions. For WhatsApp-first funnels, use the **Click-to-WhatsApp** campaign objective, since it matches both the product's own delivery mechanism and this audience's existing behavior.

**Where to send prospects:**
- **Primary recommendation: Click-to-WhatsApp ads.** This is the most natural fit — the product itself lives on WhatsApp, so sending prospects there to "experience" a mini version of the assistant (or talk to a real rep) reinforces the pitch immediately and lowers friction for a non-technical audience.
- **Secondary: Instant Forms** for broader top-of-funnel prospecting where you want to capture contact info at lower friction and follow up manually/via WhatsApp afterward.
- **Landing page** as a supporting asset (not the primary destination) for retargeting audiences who want more detail (feature breakdown, more demo videos) before committing to a WhatsApp conversation or call.
- **Booking page** reserved for warm/retargeted leads who have already engaged with WhatsApp or the landing page — asking for a booked call too early, from cold traffic, adds unnecessary friction.

**Suggested lead-qualification flow:**
1. Prospect clicks ad → lands in WhatsApp (or fills instant form).
2. Automated opener asks 2–3 light qualifying questions: restaurant/café name, type of food business, roughly how many WhatsApp/phone orders they get per day.
3. Based on answers, either continues with an automated mini-demo experience or flags the conversation for a human rep to take over and offer the free custom-menu demo.
4. Rep follows up to collect the actual menu photo and schedule the personalized demo delivery.

**Retargeting ideas:**
- Retarget video viewers (50%+ watch) with a **product-demo or carousel ad** showing more depth (dashboard, multi-branch, analytics) — they've already engaged with the problem, now show more of the solution.
- Retarget website/landing-page visitors who didn't convert with a **testimonial or founder-style ad** to build trust before asking again.
- Retarget people who opened a WhatsApp conversation but didn't complete the qualifying flow with a lighter, direct **offer-focused ad** ("Still want to see your free demo?").

**Follow-up messaging after a demo request:**
- Immediate automated confirmation acknowledging the request and setting expectations ("We'll have your custom demo ready within [X] — here's what happens next").
- A short human follow-up within 24 hours to collect the menu photo/details if not already provided.
- A scheduled nudge (2–3 days later) if the prospect goes quiet after requesting a demo, offering a specific time to walk through it live.

**Filtering out low-quality or irrelevant leads:**
- Use qualifying questions in the WhatsApp opener/instant form (business type, order volume) to filter out non-restaurant inquiries and pure browsers.
- Require a menu photo or restaurant name before booking a call — a real prospect will provide this easily; low-intent leads often won't follow through.
- Track and deprioritize leads who don't respond to the first qualifying message within a set window, rather than spending manual outreach time chasing cold form-fills.
